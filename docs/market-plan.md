# Pebble Math — the market, planned

Where each maths idea lives, what a child handles, and roughly which school year it fits. Written 18 October 2026.

**Scope:** kindergarten to Primary 3. Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, fractions, money. Primary 4 and above is out of scope — the age picker should say so plainly rather than offering something that isn't there.

**A caution on year levels.** The mapping below is a sensible reading of what children at each stage are doing, but it has not been checked against the current NERDC scheme of work. Check it before it goes in front of a school.

---

## The rule that holds the whole market together

**Every stall owns one operation, and every stall brings back an earlier one with different objects.**

The bakery teaches division, but a child still has to make a ten out of buns. The bus stop teaches addition and subtraction, but the numbers come from money earlier in the day.

If an idea only ever appears with one object, the child learns the object, not the idea. That is the difference between a child who can do 8 + 5 in your game and a child who can do it in a shop.

## The seven places

### 1. Fruit Stall — counting and addition
**Year:** KG to Primary 1 · **Status:** built, and serves as the tutorial

Oranges into a basket that holds ten. Make ten first, then count what's left over. Already done.

### 2. Danfo Bus Stop — addition and subtraction together
**Year:** Primary 1 to 2 · **Objects:** passengers, seats

Passengers get on and off. How many on the bus now? The shortest, most repeatable activity in the market, and the one that works best as a quick session. Local, funny, and the seats make a natural ten-frame.

*Brings back:* making ten, as a full bus.

### 3. Provision Store — subtraction, by cracking a crate
**Year:** Primary 1 to 2 · **Objects:** tins, sachets, bread, soap

A customer takes things off the shelf. What's left?

**The mechanic returns: crack the crate.** A child has 3 crates of ten and 2 loose tins, and a customer wants 15. There aren't enough loose ones, so they tap a crate and it breaks open.

They met this at the Fruit Stall with oranges. Here it is tins, a different shop, a different customer — the same idea. That is the rule of this market working as intended: an idea a child only ever meets with one object is an idea stuck to that object.

Two kinds of subtraction worth meeting separately: taking away, and finding the difference between two shelves.

*Brings back:* cracking a crate, from Chapter 1.

### 4. Bookshop — multiplication as equal groups
**Year:** Primary 2 to 3 · **Objects:** pencils in packs, exercise books in bundles

Not times tables. Four packs of five pencils, laid out as a grid the child builds themselves. The array is the point: it shows why 4 × 5 and 5 × 4 give the same answer, which a table never does.

*Brings back:* repeated addition from the fruit stall.

### 5. Bakery — division as fair sharing, and halves
**Year:** Primary 2 to 3 · **Objects:** buns, plates, a cake

Share buns onto plates. Spot the unfair plate. Then cut one cake in half, and in half again. Sharing and fractions are the same idea met from two sides, which is why they belong in one place.

*Brings back:* multiplication — if each plate has 3 and there are 4 plates, how many buns did we start with?

### 6. Cloth Stall — fractions and measurement
**Year:** Primary 3 · **Objects:** cloth, ribbon, a measuring stick, a tape

Cut a length in half, in quarters, in thirds. Cloth is better than cake for fractions because it stretches in one direction only, so the child sees that a third is *shorter*, not just *another piece*.

This is also where measurement lives, and it has its own order:

1. **Compare before naming.** Which ribbon is longer? No units at all.
2. **Measure with something.** How many hand-spans long is this cloth? Different hands give different answers, which is *why* a standard unit exists.
3. **Then the metre.** A metre stick that stays the same every time. Centimetres as the small marks on it.
4. **Then the kilometre**, and the only honest way to show one: not a picture, but a distance a child knows. The walk to the next stall is 20 metres; a kilometre is that walk fifty times. Scale is felt, not drawn.

*Brings back:* fair sharing from the bakery.

### 7. Money Corner — naira, kobo and change
**Year:** Primary 2 to 3 · **Objects:** coins, notes, a wallet, a savings pot

Pay for something. Work out the change. Compare two prices. Save towards something you want. This is where every earlier operation gets a consequence, and where the game's name finally pays off.

**Change by counting up, never by borrowing.** Something costs ₦68 and the customer pays ₦100. Instead of 100 − 68 with its row of borrowed zeros, the child counts forward: ₦2 to reach a friendly ₦70, then ₦30 to reach ₦100. Change is ₦32.

This is worth insisting on because it is what market traders actually do. A child who learns it here is learning the real-world method, not the classroom one, and it rests on making a ten again.

*Brings back:* all of it.

## What a child handles, by place

Objects matter as much as operations. A child who only ever drags oranges learns oranges.

| Place | Things to pick up |
|---|---|
| Fruit Stall | oranges, bananas, mangoes, baskets, crates |
| Bus Stop | passengers, seats, tickets |
| Provision Store | tins, sachets, bread, soap, a shelf |
| Bookshop | pencils, exercise books, rulers, packs |
| Bakery | buns, plates, a cake, a knife |
| Cloth Stall | cloth, ribbon, a measuring stick, scissors |
| Money Corner | coins, notes, a wallet, a savings pot |

## The progress meter

Not a score, and not coins. A small meter sits on screen. Each correct answer sends a few sparkles into it. When it fills — at the end of a chapter — the chapter is done, and the trader comes back from asking along the row to lend a lantern ([story.md](story.md)).

Why this shape:

- It rewards **finishing**, not being fast and not being first.
- The sparkles travelling from the object to the meter tell the child *that* something happened, without a message interrupting them.
- Friends are not prizes for filling it. One of Blob's friends joins at each new place, and how many have joined is how close the child is to finding their mother, not a stream of small rewards.
- It needs no second currency, so the naira in the Money Corner stays the only money in the game.

The monsters are Blob's friends from around the market, joining the search. One joins at each new place, so by the end of the market the child has met all seven and the whole market is looking. Blob is the first, there from the first minute. He is not a guide: he is lost too, he doesn't know the answers, and he is a friend rather than a teacher.

## Number facts by year

A rough map of what a child should be able to do, to aim each stall at. **Not checked against the NERDC scheme — verify before this goes near a school.**

**Kindergarten.** Count to 20. Recognise and write numerals. More, fewer, the same. Sorting by one property.

**Primary 1.** Numbers to 100. Add and subtract within 20. Make ten. Halves. Naira coins by sight.

**Primary 2.** Numbers to 1000. Add and subtract two-digit numbers with regrouping. Multiply by 2, 3, 4 and 5 as equal groups. Halves and quarters. Money and simple change. Length measured in hand-spans and steps before any units.

**Primary 3.** Numbers to 10,000. Multiplication facts to 10. Division as fair sharing, with remainders. Halves, thirds and quarters. Money with change. Metres and centimetres. Telling the time.

**Primary 4 and above.** Out of scope. The age picker should say so rather than offering a band with nothing behind it.

## Open questions

- **Side views.** Every stall currently faces front. A market where some stalls sit left and right would feel like a place rather than a backdrop. Worth one experiment before committing to seven front-facing scenes.
- **The dashboard.** It is bare because nothing has been played yet. Once stalls produce data, it needs: which children, when they last played, which places are open, and — the useful part — where a child keeps getting stuck. Streaks, a store, pets and loot are all possible later, but none of them should arrive before the dashboard answers "is my child learning anything".
- **Avatar outfits.** All six Zizi looks currently wear the same clothes. Six distinct outfits, including the parasol one, would make the picker feel like a choice rather than a hairstyle menu.
- **Animation.** No longer still: sparkles travel from a correct move into the meter, the meter fills and glows, a crate cracks open, Grandma walks off and comes back, a lantern arrives, a chosen card hops. Small motions that tell a child something happened. What is still open is the map: the haze lifting as places open.

---

## Not in this plan, on purpose

Coins as a second currency, pets, loot, leaderboards, timers. The naira in the Money Corner is the only money this game needs, and it is the thing being taught.
