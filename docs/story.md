# Pebble Math — the story

Everything in the game answers to this. If a feature does not fit here, it is
the feature that is wrong.

---

## What happened

A child came to the market with their mother. In the crowd they lost her.

Nobody took her. Nobody is hiding her. The market is simply very big and very
full, and a child who does not know it cannot see more than a few stalls in any
direction.

Blob was lost too. They found each other, which is how the two of them met.

Blob does not know where the mother is. He is not a guide and he is not clever.
He is a friend who is in the same trouble, and he says the one useful thing he
knows: **"I have friends in this market. They will help us look."**

## The haze

The haze is what being lost looks like. It is not magic and it is not evil.

Past the few stalls a child knows, the market is a blur. As they learn their way
around it, the blur pulls back. A lantern lets them see a little further into
it.

Nothing in this game is defeated. The haze lifts because the child comes to know
the place.

## Why a lost child is selling oranges

Because the traders will help them look, **and a trader cannot leave their
stall.**

So the child minds the stall and the trader goes and asks along the row. That is
the trade, and it is what a market would really do. Every chapter is part of the
search, never a detour from it.

Grandma lends them her lantern on the first day. That is where the first one
comes from.

## The sentence underneath everything

**You find your mother by becoming useful.**

## The ending

Seven friends. The haze gone. And the mother is found **because the whole market
was looking** — not because the child solved one last puzzle.

She had been searching for them the entire time. That is what really happens
when a child is lost, and it is the ending that rewards everything the child
did to get there.

---

# What this settles

## Blob's voice

Blob is **not** a teacher. He does not know the answers and must never sound
like he is withholding one.

- Right: *"Hmm. Not enough loose ones. What's inside a crate?"* — a friend
  thinking out loud, genuinely unsure.
- Wrong: *"Remember what we learned about crates!"* — a teacher pretending not
  to know.

Every hint in the game passes this test. If a line would only make sense coming
from someone who already knows, it is the wrong line.

## The two collections

**Friends** — one per place, Blob first. Blob's friends from around the market,
joining the search. Seven in all. Nothing is spent. This is the measure of how
close they are.

**Lanterns** — earned by helping, spent to see further into the haze and open
the next place. Lit for the ones they have, cold and dark for the ones they
need.

## How each place goes

1. A trader needs help and cannot leave the stall.
2. The child helps. (This is the maths.)
3. The trader goes to ask along the row, and lends a lantern.
4. One of Blob's friends is here, and joins them.
5. The haze pulls back. The next place appears.

## What this rules out

- **No villain.** Nothing is chasing them and nothing took the mother.
- **No lives, no timer, no failing.** A lost child is already under enough
  pressure, and there is no story reason for a clock.
- **No reward for being fast or right first time.** Children who find it hard
  are not less deserving of help finding their mother.
- **Nothing collected that does not lead to her.** Trophies belong in a
  different game.
